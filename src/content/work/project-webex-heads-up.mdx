# Cisco Webex: Heads Up

## Summary

‘Heads Up’ is a meeting app feature we designed for Webex as an intensive capstone project for the School of Visual Concepts.

With the support of two designers, I worked with Webex’s design team to unpack Webex’s business problem and created ‘Heads Up’: a low-effort Webex feature to alert disruptors during real-time meetings, and reduce meeting fatigue.

Our prototype **really excited** the Webex Design team with something to consider in their next feature review. When we shared it with our usability testers and peer reviewers, **every single one of them said they'd love a tool like Heads Up**.

<Eyebrow>Problem</Eyebrow>

## Too many meetings + disruptions = <Hl c="orange">Meeting fatigue</Hl>.

<Rule />

When people just want to get work done, meeting disruptions are the last thing they want in the way of it.

<Img src="webex-heads-up/frame-1546.png" alt="" caption="Since when did lecture slides look like Airbnb cabins?" />

Like when the presenter accidentally shares the wrong screen, or when a rogue mic’s background noise disrupts the conversation for everyone.

Those are a few of the many meeting disruptions people experience, compounded by the millions of daily video meetings. **They are usually accidental, but always annoying or embarrassing,** and ultimately derail meetings from becoming productive.

<Labeled label="User problem">

### What is<Hl c="orange">meeting fatigue</Hl>?

<Img src="webex-heads-up/frame-1563.png" alt="" caption={"Meeting Fatigue = \"Not my problem\""} />

Meeting participants are worn from back-to-back meetings and have mentally clocked-out. <br /><br />You’re physically exhausted, with mics muted and cameras off. You’re mentally clocked out and distracted from the meeting. Lastly, you’re also apathetic to others via the Bystander effect, where you’re less likely to engage or help alert a disruptor in the presence of other people.

</Labeled>

<Labeled label="Business problem">

### <Hl c="orange">Meeting fatigue</Hl> = Diminished employee productivity.

And here’s the kicker, when meeting participants, like employees, are meeting fatigued, they cost companies money.

Companies lose on productivity, employee disengagement, and weaken customer relationships.

<Img src="webex-heads-up/3-item-module.png" alt="" caption="Source: Webex (2023)" />

</Labeled>

</Section>

### 01.<br />❌ Users really don’t like meeting disruptions...

Real-time disruptions (_think:_ **_hot mics, choppy connections, buggy screen shares,_** _to name a few..._) lead to significant meeting fatigue.

<Grid cols={2} reveal="right">

<div>

### 02.<br />❌ …but users don’t like disrupting the disruptor, either.

**This is because a majority of meeting participants are bystanders, or ‘passive users’,**<br /><br />They prefer to gather information and remain silent or multi-task throughout the meeting.

</div>

<div>

<Img src="webex-heads-up/frame-1496.png" alt="" caption="Source: mmhmm (n=1000)" />

<div>

### 03.<br />🌟 Non-verbal tools engage the disengaged.

Interviewees resoundingly prefer expressing engagement through non-verbal tools than by unmuting. <br /><br />**Non-verbal tools are like chat and reactions – low effort, low barriers to entry tools that offer very high engagement during meetings.**<br /><br />Not only do users prefer this, but an overwhelming majority believe it will increase productivity. <br /><br />Non-verbal tools are a key opportunity to actively combat real-time meeting passivity and elevate the user experience.

<Eyebrow>Wireframing</Eyebrow>

## Helping meeting hosts and participants be more productive.

<Rule />

To ensure our wireframes aligned, we thoroughly studied [Cisco's Momentum Design system](https://momentum.design/), branding guidelines, and content guidelines. <br /><br />I then worked with our web developer, Andrew, to wireframe designs and achieve three key goals:

<Grid cols={2}>

<div>

<Img src="webex-heads-up/frame-1538.png" alt="" caption="Keep it intuitive in its function and findability." />

</div>

<div>

### Find the disruptor and alert, all in one drawer.

**Non-verbal tool:** I grouped the Heads Up tool with other non-verbal tools to leverage current mental models of silent and non-verbal tools that offer high engagement with minimal effort**<br /><br />Flag key meeting disruptions:** Disruptions are a key contributor to meeting fatigue, so I added common meeting disruptions and added them as alerts. 

</div>

</Grid>

<Grid cols={2} reveal="right">

<div>

### "Heads Up!"

**Minimize distress in urgent situations:** I worked with our content designer to highlight concise and informative content, and present clear call-to-action resolutions.**<br /><br />Account for those who are distracted:** I designed a secondary 'Toast' notification to display outside of the meeting window to ensure they see a notification

</div>

<div>

<Img src="webex-heads-up/frame-1539.png" alt="" caption="Disruptors see a notification even if they're distracted." />

</div>

</Grid>

<Grid cols={2}>

<div>

<Img src="webex-heads-up/frame-1540.png" alt="" caption="Create a need for Hosts to activate 'Heads Up'." />

</div>

<div>

### Empower meeting hosts.

**Host privileges are more powerful:** Hosts receive notifications about disruption alerts, and can utilize their Host privileges (ex: Mute) to maintain meeting control.**<br /><br />Intuitive to understand and activate:** I prioritized concise tooltips to clarify its functionality. It's seamlessly activated in the Meeting Options window.

## 💎 Small ideas can deliver the most value.

Tackling the broad problem of meeting fatigue naturally encouraged us to imagine wild ideas like ‘_AI_’ this, and ‘_giant feature_’ that, but I learned that great solutions are those that benefit both the user and the company. <br /><br />While the ‘Heads Up’ idea I proposed was not the flashiest solution, I found it solved the user and business problems with the most value and feasibility.

## 🚀 Poke holes. Be Agile.

If you’re still in the discovery and define phase and think you’re narrowing in and have your idea figured out, don’t get comfortable with it. <br /><br />Welcome yourself and your team to keep throwing things against the wall and poking holes and putting up sticky notes.
