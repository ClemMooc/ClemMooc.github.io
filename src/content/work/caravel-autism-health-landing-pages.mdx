# Caravel Autism Health: facility pages that stay accurate

Caravel runs 40+ clinics for children with autism. Parents couldn't trust the clinic pages, which were often out of date, and the key actions were buried. As the project lead at Yext, a cloud software company, I redesigned the pages and moved every clinic's data into one source of truth. Now one update in that knowledge graph reaches the pages and 70+ publishers, including Google, Facebook, Apple, WebMD, and CareDash.

<Eyebrow>Problem</Eyebrow>

## Two problems: buried information and stale data

**Vital information was below the fold.** In kickoff, Caravel's VPs told me many visits ended without a single key click. Our research with other healthcare clients showed parents come to do three things: contact the clinic, get directions, and learn about the staff. My heuristic review found all three near the bottom of the page.

**Every change was manual.** Caravel updated hours and staff on Google, Facebook, and other listings first, and the website days later or not at all. Out-of-date pages eroded trust and pushed parents to call clinics with questions the page should have answered.

<Eyebrow>What I did</Eyebrow>

## Redesign the page, centralize the data, keep everyone on schedule

**1. Put the important things above the fold.** I reviewed competitor and peer healthcare sites to see which information modules they used and how they ordered them. Then I designed modules that put contact details, directions, and the clinic's people first, and added an FAQ module to head off routine calls. I ran **weekly design reviews** with Caravel's team to agree the page structure, visual hierarchy, and the copy and media each of the 40+ pages needed.

**2. Make one source of truth.** I worked with Caravel's data team to load every clinic into our platform's **central knowledge graph**, with custom fields for staff, service areas, and FAQs. Now when, for example, the Green Bay clinic changes its hours, one edit updates the facility page and every connected listing.

**3. Run the delivery.** With a fixed launch date, I ran weekly syncs with status slides and due dates, and used a content matrix so Caravel knew exactly which copy and images we needed from them, and when.
