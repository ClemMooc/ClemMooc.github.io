# GymMate: three gym tools in one app

GymMate is a workout tracker, warmup calculator and rest timer I planned, tested and shipped for my own training. I use it every session, and so do two lifting friends. [Try the demo](https://warm-up-and-lift.lovable.app/demo), no account needed.

<Eyebrow>Why I built it</Eyebrow>

## Three touchpoints for every set

<Grid cols={2}>

<div>

I've lifted for several years, built around 5x5 training and progressive overload: add a little weight each session and keep the streak going. That only works if you know exactly what you lifted last time.

My setup had three touchpoints:

1. **A Google Sheets log.** I built each workout by hand, copying and pasting rows of sets and reps every session.
2. **A warmup calculator website** to work out which plates to load for each warmup set.
3. **A rest timer** on my phone or Apple Watch.

Every switch cost focus between sets. I wanted one place that remembers my last session, plans my warmups and times my rest.

</div>

<Eyebrow>How I built it</Eyebrow>

## Plan first, then test it under the bar

**1. A detailed spec before any code.** I used an AI app builder, and I knew the first prompt sets the app's foundation. A vague start means endless fix-and-break loops later. Think of building a house: asking for "a single-story house with a basement" up front beats adding the basement after the house is built. So my first prompt asked only for a code design plan, and explicitly ruled out writing code until the plan was solid.

<Accordion title="What went into the spec">

I wrote the spec with a structured "prompt wizard," then ran it past other AI tools to find gaps before building:

- **Purpose:** what the app is for and who it serves. The most important part, since every later decision traces back to it.
- **Look and feel:** visual direction, starting from a Figma Make mockup.
- **Known UI elements:** the screens, tabs, buttons and fields it needs.
- **User flows:** the full path through a workout, including what every button does.
- **Inputs and actions:** for each thing a user enters, what happens to it.
- **Mock data:** a small, realistic set (20–50 records) so the prototype stays fast.

</Accordion>

**2. Dogfooding, mine and two friends'.** I used every build in real workouts, and two friends who lift used it in theirs. Mid-set is the harshest test there is: anything slow, confusing or wrong shows up immediately.

**3. Small, focused fix lists.** Each session's notes became a short, specific list of changes. About 40 rounds from May to October 2026.

</Section>

<Eyebrow>What it does</Eyebrow>

## Remembers, calculates, times, then gets out of the way

<Grid cols={2} reveal="right">

<div>

- **Remembers your last session.** Add an exercise and it fills in last time's weight, sets and reps.
- **Calculates warmups.** Pick bench, squat or deadlift, enter your working weight, and it lays out each warmup set with the plates to load per side.
- **Times your rest** between sets, no second device needed.
- **Routines and history.** Load a saved routine in one tap; check any exercise's history before you lift.
- **Saves your progress.** Sign in with SSO and it syncs across phone and laptop; it also installs to your home screen.
- **Settings that matter.** Accessibility options, plus a spreadsheet export laid out like my original Google Sheets log.

</div>

<Eyebrow>What I learned</Eyebrow>

### Plan before you build

The time I spent on the spec paid off every week after. A thorough foundation meant fixes stayed small instead of turning into rebuilds.

### Daily use finds what reviews miss

Using it mid-workout, with tired hands and 90 seconds of rest, surfaced problems no desk review would have.

### Small, specific changes win

Short, focused fix lists shipped cleanly. Big batches of changes were where things broke.
