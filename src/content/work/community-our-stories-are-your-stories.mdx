# Our Stories Are Your Stories (OSAYS)

<Button href="https://www.ourstoriesareyourstories.com/">OSAYS Website</Button>

## Project Summary

The increase in anti-Asian racism and xenophobia has opened deep wounds in the AANHPI community and served as a painful reminder of the persistence of discrimination. In honor of AANHPI Heritage Month, we launched this campaign to support the AANHPI community to remind them they are not alone and that they are seen; and illuminate the AANHPI experience to dispel misconceptions and celebrate the representation from such a wide range of cultures. <br /><br />Being involved with OSAYS all began from writing a blog post for Success Cohorts: [I Am The Proud Son Of Refugees](https://www.successcohorts.com/blog/summer-retreat-te8j4). Betti Fujikado, the co-founder of Success Cohorts, encouraged me to talk about my unique Asian experiences in Our Stories Are Your Stories, to illuminate the AANHPI experience and celebrate the representation from such a wide range of Asian cultures.

<Eyebrow>OSAYS: Success Cohorts (2023)</Eyebrow>

Success Cohorts was an incredible program. As a member of the initial Alpha cohort, Success Cohort equipped me with valuable skills, gave me the confidence to land my full-time job, and paired me with incredible connections.

We all met during the pandemic, and as recent grads, most of us felt disillusioned entering quarantine amidst a historically bad job market. Motivation was drained. Success Cohorts helped to recalibrate myself. The bi-weekly sessions were always engaging and taught me valuable lessons, ranging from: setting goals, being accountable, and growing your network.I have grown significantly since that first meeting.

I credit the collaborative learning environments, the infectious energy from Betti, Carla, and Linnea, and the words of wisdom from my mentor Korey.

</Section>

<Section tone="dark">

<Video vimeo="561430519" title="Our Stories Are Your Stories video" />

<Eyebrow>OSAYS: David and Karen (2021)</Eyebrow>

This was a labor of love. I spent 3 weeks with my friend MaryAnne, with **0 video skills**, and **with a $0 budget**, to turn what was originally an essay into a video for OSAYS and the Wing Luke Museum. I’ve learned to be more empathetic to what my parents went through from listening to their childhood during the war.

I believe it was cathartic for my parents to share their stories with their family and their children, but to the Asian community as well. I feel this experience helped us to understand what being a “refugee” really means, and instilled the significance of food in our family. I also felt more connected with my extended family, as I collaborated with family members across Washington, California, and France, who all ran through their photo albums to send photographs to include in the video. Once the video was released, they were the first ones to reach out and mention how prideful they were that their story was being represented.

I’m grateful I had the opportunity to provide a voice for my family during AAPI Heritage month--especially during the height of the Stop AAPI Hate movement.

</Section>
