# Lunar Crafts & Designs: making Chinese festivals easier to learn and shop for

My first end-to-end project. As a first-generation Asian American and the child of Vietnam War refugees, I'd struggled to buy things like gold burning paper (金紙) and red envelopes (紅包) without knowing how they're used. I designed a store that teaches the traditions as you shop. The result is a clickable web and iOS prototype, validated rather than launched: six usability tests drove three major redesigns.

<Eyebrow>Problem</Eyebrow>

## Eager to celebrate, but lost in translation

Chinese New Year is one of the world's most celebrated festivals, yet many Chinese Americans can't easily take part. Language barriers cut them off from older relatives who know the traditions. Online, they piece answers together from translation apps and cluttered guides. In stores, festival goods sit in a generic "ethnic" aisle with labels they can't read.

<Img src="lunar-crafts/user-problem.png" alt="Three photos of festival product packaging run through camera translation apps, producing confusing, garbled English." caption="Camera translation apps raise more questions than they answer." />

**How might we** demystify Chinese festivals and festival shopping for native English speakers, so they can host their own?

</Section>

<Section tone="light">

<Eyebrow>What I did</Eyebrow>

## Research, then design, then three rounds of fixes

**1. Research.** I wrote a research plan, **surveyed 30 Asian Americans** who wanted to know more about their heritage, and interviewed people who had celebrated at least one Chinese festival. I also analyzed six competitors to find the gap: they offered little about the traditions behind what they sold.

What I learned:

- **Motivated, not confident.** 91% of respondents wanted to celebrate their family heritage, but 67% didn't feel confident hosting or taking part in a festival.
- **No trusted source.** People pieced together answers from translation apps, websites, and family, and weren't sure they were right.
- **Language was the #1 pain point,** both for learning and for reading product packaging.
- **They like supporting small businesses.** Two in three said supporting minority cultures was their main reason to buy from a minority-owned shop.

<Callout by="Interview participant">

"Now that Asian culture is more widely accepted in America, it's important to embrace it."

</Callout>

**Market context.** [The US Asian population nearly doubled (88%) between 2000 and 2019](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2021/04/29/key-facts-about-asian-americans/), and [Chinese Americans are the largest Asian-origin group](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2021/04/29/key-facts-about-asian-americans/). [81% of Asian Americans said violence against them was increasing](http://pewresearch.org/short-reads/2022/05/09/about-a-third-of-asian-americans-say-they-have-changed-their-daily-routine-due-to-concerns-over-threats-attacks/), and [88% want more Asian American representation on screen](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2023/after-oscars-asian-american-viewers-look-to-advertisers-media-more-representation/). [56% of Americans intend to be more aware of other cultures' holidays](https://www.pollfish.com/blog/original-insights/cultural-awareness-of-foreign-holidays-from-around-the-world/), and [arts and crafts have risen among the most-traded cultural goods](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/cultural-goods-economic-driver-digital-age).

<Img src="lunar-crafts/lunar-crafts-image.png" alt="Logos of six competitor stores that were analyzed" caption="Six direct competitors analyzed." />

**What competitors did well:** a strong identity and AAPI mission, and good craft selections.
**The gaps:** little learning material about festivals and rituals, weak language support, poor discoverability, and small vendors' stories left untold.

</Accordion>

**2. Design.** The goals: help users learn about and prepare for festivals easily, and help the business sell artisanal goods from small Asian-owned shops. I sketched key screens on paper, explored alternatives, then built low-fidelity wireframes in Figma.

<Accordion title="Design detail: wireframes">

**3. Test and fix.** I ran **6 usability tests** with Asian Americans who had celebrated a Chinese festival and wanted to learn more. Three changes came out of them:

- **The product page was cluttered.** People missed the features that would have helped them. I regrouped it with clearer labels and stronger hierarchy.
- **Learning modules were hard to read.** I made the hierarchy consistent and increased type sizes.
- **Some category pages mixed buying and learning.** I cut the learning module down so the page clearly read as a shop.

<Section tone="light">

<Eyebrow>What I learned</Eyebrow>

### Every test shows you something you didn't know

Each usability session surfaced something I hadn't thought of. It's easy to assume a design works until someone tries it.

### Above-the-fold space sets the purpose

When secondary content takes the top of a page, people can't tell what the page is for. Keep the user's main goal up top.
